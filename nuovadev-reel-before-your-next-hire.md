## The creative angle (and why it fits NuovaDev)

**Angle: "The 9:47 PM enquiry."** The reel follows a single enquiry twice. The first time it arrives late in the evening, gets copied by hand from inbox to spreadsheet to CRM, and is answered two days later, after the customer has moved on. Then the same enquiry arrives at the same time and goes through a connected system, which replies, books a call and logs the details while the team is offline.

Why this angle:

- **It names a decision only buyers make.** The hook, "Don't hire another person to fix this," speaks to the person who approves headcount. Employees and hobbyists scroll past, but founders, CEOs and owners recognise a choice they're facing. That pre-qualifies viewers, which matters more than raw views for a B2B service.
- **One problem covers most of your audience.** Service firms, agencies, e-commerce stores, restaurants that take catering or bulk orders, and early-stage SaaS teams all lose enquiries this way. Viewers recognise themselves without a list of industries.
- **It sells services without listing them.** The fix naturally covers AI chatbots, AI automation and agents, connected workflows and custom internal tools. NuovaDev looks like a team that thinks in systems, not a shop that builds whatever it's asked to build.
- **It's on the team's side.** "Your team isn't the problem" stops the reel sounding like "replace your staff with AI." That keeps the tone premium and trustworthy, and it fits owners who care about their people.
- **The repeat structure does the persuading.** Showing the same enquiry at the same time with a different outcome makes the before-and-after obvious without any statistics, client results or invented claims.

**What I changed from your starting concept.** "Your business doesn't always need more employees. Sometimes, it needs better systems" is the right thesis, but it's a poor first line. It takes about 4 seconds to say and gives away the payoff before the viewer has a reason to stay. So the thesis moves to the turning point ("More people won't fix that. Better systems will."), and the opening becomes a short, contrarian line with an open loop.

## A. Final voiceover script

**Word count:** about 121 words. **Runtime:** about 44 seconds at a confident, unhurried pace.
`/` = short pause · `//` = full beat (about half a second of silence)

> **[0:00]** Don't hire another person to fix this. //
>
> **[0:03]** An enquiry lands at 9:47 PM. / Next morning, someone copies it into a spreadsheet. / Then the CRM. / Two days later, you reply. / They've moved on. //
>
> **[0:13]** Your team isn't the problem. / They've become the glue between tools that don't talk to each other. / More people won't fix that. // Better systems will.
>
> **[0:22]** Same enquiry. 9:47 PM. / An AI assistant replies in seconds, books the call, and updates your CRM. / Your team wakes up to booked calls — not a backlog.
>
> **[0:31]** That's what we build at NuovaDev. / Within 48 hours of our first call, you'll have a written scope, timeline and price.
>
> **[0:38]** So before your next hire — book a free 30-minute call at nuovadev.com.

**Delivery notes for the speaker**

- Talk like you're explaining this to another founder over coffee: calm and certain, never selling. Ending lines on a falling tone sounds confident; a rising tone sounds unsure.
- Start the first word within 0.2 seconds of frame 1. Cut any breath or "um" before it.
- "They've moved on." Say it flat and quiet, then let the silence work.
- "Better systems will." Pause before it and land it like a full stop. This is the line people should remember.
- "That's what we build at NuovaDev." Allow a slight smile here. This is the first time the brand appears, so it should feel like a reveal rather than a pitch.
- Say "nuovadev.com" slowly and clearly, and make sure the domain is on screen at that exact moment.
- Pronounce "NuovaDev" the way your team does, and keep it the same in every video. Stress the "nuo" clearly. Similarly named companies exist (NovaDev, Nuova Soft), so a viewer who hears "nova" may type the wrong domain. That's another reason the domain must be on screen whenever it's spoken.
- For a US-weighted audience, change "enquiry" to "inquiry" in both the voiceover and the text.

## E. Editing direction

### Visual style and colour palette

The look should be calm, minimal and cinematic: closer to a product launch film than an ad.

- **Brand colours come first.** Take the primary accent from NuovaDev's site and logo. If you need a starting point, use these placeholders:
  - Base: graphite `#0F1115` · Surface: `#1A1D23` · Text: off-white `#F4F4F1` · Muted: `#8A8F98`
  - Accent: one brand colour, used for the "Better systems" text, the connecting lines and the CTA (placeholder blue `#4C7DFF`)
  - Warning: amber `#F2A93B`, used **once**, on "2 days later"
- **Grade it as two worlds.** The "before" section is cool, slightly desaturated, dim and lit by screen light. The "after" section is clean and bright, with warm window light and the brand accent. Viewers should feel the before-and-after even with the sound off.
- **Keep mock UIs generic.** Build them in Figma with no third-party logos, real names or prices. They should read as an illustration, not a case study.
- **Avoid:** glowing circuit boards, hooded hackers, robots, handshake stock footage, sci-fi heads-up displays, AI-generated faces or hands, and lens flares.

### Pacing and cuts

- **Problem (0:03–0:13):** a new shot every 1–2 seconds. It should feel busy but controlled.
- **Insight (0:13–0:22):** slow down. Hold on the face, use silence, and let the graphic build. This contrast keeps attention.
- **Solution (0:22–0:31):** rhythmic, one UI step per beat.
- **Brand and CTA (0:31–0:44):** calm and steady, with the longest holds in the video.
- Never leave a frame completely still for more than about 3 seconds. Use a slow push-in or parallax at minimum.

### Captions and typography

- **One typeface family, two weights.** Inter, Geist or Manrope are free and clean. Use semibold for captions and bold for headlines.
- **Captions:** burned in, 3–5 words per chunk, maximum 2 lines, sentence case, about 60–64 px on a 1080-wide canvas. Use white with a soft shadow of about 20%. Highlight **one** key word per chunk in the accent colour: *hire, 9:47, glue, systems, seconds, NuovaDev, 48 hours, free*.
- **Headline overlays:** 80–96 px bold for the hook, thesis and CTA. Use 40–48 px for small labels such as "Next morning."
- Proofread the automatic captions for "enquiry," "CRM," "NuovaDev" and "nuovadev.com." Caption tools often get these wrong.

### Transitions

- About 90% hard cuts.
- **Match cuts:** pasted cell to CRM field, and phone at 0:03 to the identical phone at 0:22.
- **One signature move each:** the snap at 0:21 and the rewind at 0:22. Nothing else.
- No spins, glitches, light leaks, zoom blurs or 3D cube transitions.

### Background music

- Minimal, modern electronic with a steady pulse at around 100–115 BPM. Think sparse synth, soft kick and clean keys, not "epic corporate."
- **Arc:** a low pulse during the problem → **complete silence** at 0:11–0:15 → a pad returns → **the beat drops on "Better systems will"** → full groove through the solution → resolves under the brand and CTA.
- Keep the music roughly 15–20 dB under the voice.
- Business accounts often have a limited in-app music library. License a track (for example from Artlist, Epidemic Sound or Musicbed) and mix it into the export, or use Meta's Sound Collection.

### Sound effects

Soft, generic notification ticks (not a recognisable branded phone sound), keyboard clacks, copy/paste key clicks, a soft send whoosh, a muted low thud on "They've moved on," UI pops, a crisp snap on "Better systems," a subtle reverse-whoosh, typing-dot and message sounds, a calendar click, light morning ambience, and a final tick that loops into the opening.

### Holding attention in the first three seconds

1. **Frame 1 does three things at once:** a face, notifications already moving, and readable text. Nothing fades in.
2. **The voice starts within 0.2 seconds.** Trim the breath.
3. **Sound on frame 1:** the notification ticks act as an audio hook for viewers with sound on.
4. **No logo and no "Hi, we're NuovaDev."** The brand is earned at 0:31.
5. **Frame 1 must work as a still image,** because some people see it as a thumbnail before it plays.
6. **The claim is contrarian and aimed at owners,** so the viewer has to ask "fix *what*?"
7. **The loop:** the end tick runs into the opening tick, which encourages a rewatch.

### Looking premium on a small budget

- **Light:** shoot the presenter facing a window, with the light falling across the face at about 45°. Use one cheap LED panel only for the dim evening phone shot.
- **Audio is the biggest upgrade:** a clip-on lavalier or a small wireless mic. Poor audio feels cheaper than poor video.
- **Camera:** a recent phone in 4K on a tripod at eye level. Use the same frame rate throughout (25 or 30 fps) and lock exposure and focus.
- **Set:** a plain wall or a tidy desk with one plant. Solid-colour clothing with no logos. Use the same location for every presenter shot so the series stays consistent.
- **Screens:** build every UI in Figma, animate with Smart Animate or simple keyframes, and record at high resolution. Use smooth eased zooms (Screen Studio, or auto-zoom in CapCut or Premiere).
- **Restraint:** one font, one accent colour, two transitions and no stock clichés. Consistency is most of what makes a video look premium.
- **Export:** 1080×1920, H.264, high bitrate. In the Instagram app, turn on **Upload at highest quality** (Settings → Data usage and media quality).

## G. Conversion strategy

### Primary CTA: book the free 30-minute call

Use one action everywhere: the spoken voiceover, the end card, the caption, the bio and Stories.

It suits this reel for three reasons:

- **The reel has already qualified the viewer.** Anyone still watching at 0:38 has recognised the problem. The next step should be a conversation about *their* version of it, not a newsletter or a follow.
- **The risk is low.** It's free, it's 30 minutes, and the viewer knows exactly what they get afterwards: a written scope, timeline and price within 48 hours. Naming what happens after the call is the strongest conversion detail in the script.
- **It fits the positioning.** A call where you listen first is the "business first, then code" promise in action.

The DM keyword ("SYSTEM") in the pinned comment is a **secondary, lower-pressure route** for people not ready to book. Its job is to move them to the same booking link.

### The path from viewer to enquiry

**Reel → profile → bio link → booking page → short form → calendar → confirmation**

1. **Bio (≤150 characters):** align it with the reel for the campaign period. For example:
   `Websites, apps, AI automation & custom software for growing businesses. Free 30-min call → written scope, timeline & price in 48h ↓`
2. **Bio link:** send people **straight to the consultation booking page, not the homepage.** Everyone who taps should be one tap away from booking. Tag the link so you can see what Instagram drives:
   `?utm_source=instagram&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=before-next-hire&utm_content=reel`
3. **Booking page or form:** match the reel's language. These are recommendations to adapt to what's already on your site.
   - **Headline:** "Missed enquiries aren't a hiring problem." Subheading: "Book a free 30-minute call. Within 48 hours, you'll have a written scope, timeline and price."
   - **Five fields at most:** name, work email, company or website, **"What's slipping through or taking too long right now?"** (open text), and timeline (optional).
   - That open-text question is your qualifier. It filters out casual browsers and gives you material to prepare a sharper call.
   - **"What happens next" in three steps:** 30-minute call → written scope, timeline and price within 48 hours → you decide.
   - **Trust block,** using only verified points from your site: you own your code, accounts, domain and data; working demos every two weeks; one team from build through launch, hosting and support. Don't add testimonials or logos unless they're real and you have permission to use them.
   - **Mobile first:** most visitors arrive through Instagram's in-app browser. Test there. Make sure it loads fast, has no pop-ups, and that the calendar works without signing in.
4. **Confirmation page and email:** repeat the promise ("You'll have a written scope, timeline and price within 48 hours of our call"). Ask them to bring one example of where things slip through, so the call starts with their real problem.

### Handling DMs and comments

- Reply to comments in the first hour after posting, with real answers rather than "Thanks! 🔥."
- **DM reply when someone sends "SYSTEM" or asks a question:**
  1. *"Thanks for reaching out. Quick question so we point you in the right direction: where do enquiries usually get stuck for you right now — inbox, spreadsheet, CRM, or slow follow-up?"*
  2. After they answer: *"That's worth looking at properly. You can grab a free 30-minute slot here: [booking link]. After the call, you'll have a written scope, timeline and price within 48 hours."*
- Never open with a pitch. Ask first. The reel's whole promise is that NuovaDev understands the problem before recommending a solution.

### The call has to match the reel

The reel promises a team that thinks in systems, so the 30-minute call should feel that way:

1. Trace one real enquiry from first contact to reply.
2. Mark the gaps where people copy, chase or forget.
3. Recommend what's worth automating, and **say plainly what isn't.** That honesty builds trust and stays with the prospect.
4. Send the written scope within the 48 hours you promised.

### Measuring what matters

- **Judge the reel by booked, qualified calls, not views.** Track profile visits, external link taps, booking-page visits (from the UTM), calls booked, and how many of those calls are a genuine fit.
- **Shares and saves** suggest it resonated with the right people. Business owners sending it to a co-founder or operations lead is the best signal you can get.
- Give it about 2 weeks, plus a few related posts, before deciding whether the angle works.

### Extending it

- **Make it a series:** "The 9:47 PM Series." Use the same format and CTA to cover other gaps, such as quote follow-ups, order-status questions, invoice chasing and client onboarding. Each episode is another chance to be the company that understands *that* problem.
- **Post the same vertical edit on LinkedIn.** Many B2B decision-makers are more active there than on Instagram.
- **If you have budget,** run the reel as an ad aimed at business owners. Then retarget people who watched most of it with a direct "Book a free 30-minute call" ad.

**No reel guarantees virality, sales or a set number of leads.** This plan aims to attract the right viewers, make the next step easy, and make the call feel worth booking.
